The Commissioner of Taxation has general administration of the income tax law and may issue public rulings that bind the Commissioner when they apply to a taxpayer. A taxpayer who relies on a public ruling is protected from shortfall penalties and interest if the ruling is later found to be incorrect.
Assessable income includes ordinary income, such as salary, wages, business income and interest, together with statutory income that is specifically included by a provision of the income tax law. Exempt income and non-assessable non-exempt income are excluded when calculating taxable income.
A general deduction is available for a loss or outgoing to the extent it is incurred in gaining or producing assessable income, or is necessarily incurred in carrying on a business for that purpose. Outgoings of a capital, private or domestic nature are not deductible under the general provision.
The goods and services tax applies to taxable supplies made for consideration in the course of an enterprise connected with the indirect tax zone. Input-taxed supplies, such as residential rent and most financial supplies, do not attract GST, and the supplier cannot claim input tax credits for acquisitions relating to them.
Single Touch Payroll requires employers to report salary or wages, pay as you go withholding and superannuation information to the tax office each time they pay employees. Employees can view their year-to-date information through their online government services account.
The superannuation guarantee charge is not deductible, unlike superannuation contributions paid on time. Employers that do not pay the charge may be issued a director penalty notice, making directors personally liable for the unpaid amount.
A trust is an obligation imposed on a trustee to deal with property for the benefit of beneficiaries. Discretionary trusts give the trustee discretion to decide which beneficiaries receive distributions of income or capital in each income year.
Division 7A treats certain payments, loans and forgiven debts provided by a private company to shareholders or their associates as unfranked dividends unless the loan is placed on complying terms before the company's lodgment day.
The National Employment Standards are eleven minimum entitlements that apply to all employees covered by the national workplace relations system. They cover maximum weekly hours, requests for flexible working arrangements, parental leave, annual leave, personal leave, community service leave, long service leave, public holidays, notice of termination and redundancy pay, the Fair Work Information Statement and offers to convert casual employment.
Modern awards set out minimum terms and conditions of employment for an industry or occupation, in addition to the National Employment Standards. They include minimum wages, penalty rates, overtime, allowances, rostering arrangements and consultation obligations. The Fair Work Commission reviews modern awards and may vary them.
Enterprise bargaining allows employers, employees and their bargaining representatives to negotiate an agreement tailored to a particular workplace. Bargaining representatives must meet good faith bargaining requirements, including attending meetings, disclosing relevant information in a timely manner and responding to proposals.
An employee is protected from unfair dismissal if they have completed the minimum employment period and either earn less than the high income threshold or are covered by an award or enterprise agreement. A dismissal is unfair if it was harsh, unjust or unreasonable and was not a case of genuine redundancy.
A genuine redundancy occurs when an employer no longer requires the employee's job to be performed by anyone because of operational changes, and the employer has complied with any consultation obligations. A dismissal is not a genuine redundancy if it would have been reasonable to redeploy the employee within the enterprise.
The general protections provisions protect workplace rights, freedom of association and freedom from discrimination. A person must not take adverse action against another person because they have a workplace right, have exercised a workplace right, or propose to exercise a workplace right.
Persons conducting a business or undertaking have a primary duty to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health and safety of workers engaged or caused to be engaged by them. Officers must exercise due diligence to ensure the business complies with its duties.
A company is a separate legal entity with the capacity and powers of an individual and of a body corporate. It can own property, enter contracts and sue or be sued in its own name. Members of a company limited by shares are liable only for any amount unpaid on their shares.
Directors and other officers must exercise their powers and discharge their duties with the degree of care and diligence that a reasonable person would exercise if they were a director or officer of a corporation in the corporation's circumstances and occupied the same office.
A director must exercise their powers and discharge their duties in good faith in the best interests of the corporation and for a proper purpose. A person who is involved in a contravention of these duties may also contravene the law as an accessory.
The financial reporting requirements in Chapter 2M of the Corporations Act apply to disclosing entities, public companies, large proprietary companies and registered schemes. Financial reports must comply with accounting standards and must be audited unless relief is available.
A company must keep written financial records that correctly record and explain its transactions and financial position and performance, and would enable true and fair financial statements to be prepared and audited. The records must be retained for seven years after the transactions covered by the records are completed.
Liquidation is the process of winding up a company's affairs, realising its assets and distributing the proceeds to creditors in the statutory order of priority. Employee entitlements such as wages, superannuation, leave and redundancy pay are generally paid ahead of ordinary unsecured creditors.
An Australian financial services licence is required to carry on a financial services business in Australia. Licensees must comply with general obligations, including having adequate resources, maintaining competence, having adequate risk management systems and a dispute resolution system that includes membership of the external complaints authority.
The market integrity rules supplement the Corporations Act by setting out requirements for market participants on licensed financial markets. Insider trading occurs when a person in possession of inside information trades in, or procures another person to trade in, the relevant financial products.
The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority supervises authorised deposit-taking institutions, general insurers, life insurers, private health insurers and most superannuation funds. Prudential standards address capital adequacy, liquidity, governance, risk management and operational resilience.
The Australian Transaction Reports and Analysis Centre regulates reporting entities that provide designated services. Reporting entities must enrol, maintain an anti-money laundering and counter-terrorism financing program, verify customer identity and report suspicious matters, threshold transactions and international funds transfer instructions.
Voluntary administration begins when a company's directors appoint an independent administrator because they believe the company is or is likely to become insolvent. The administrator investigates the company's affairs and holds a first meeting of creditors within eight business days. At the second meeting, creditors decide whether the company should execute a deed of company arrangement, go into liquidation, or be returned to the directors. During the administration a moratorium generally prevents creditors from enforcing their claims.
A public company must hold an annual general meeting at least once each calendar year and within five months after the end of its financial year. The business of the meeting usually includes considering the financial report, the directors' report and the auditor's report, electing directors and appointing the auditor. Listed companies must also put a non-binding resolution to adopt the remuneration report. If 25 percent or more of votes are cast against the report at two consecutive meetings, a spill resolution must be put to members.
Under the National Employment Standards, full-time and part-time employees accrue four weeks of paid annual leave for each year of service, with shift workers entitled to an additional week. Annual leave accumulates progressively according to ordinary hours of work and carries over from year to year. Casual employees do not receive paid annual leave but are compensated through a casual loading, which is commonly 25 percent. Many modern awards also provide an annual leave loading of 17.5 percent.
Every company director must have a director identification number, a unique 15-digit identifier that a director keeps permanently. Applications are made to the Australian Business Registry Services and must be completed personally by the director because identity verification is required. Newly appointed directors must apply before their appointment. Failing to hold a director identification number when required can attract civil and criminal penalties.
The whistleblower provisions in Part 9.4AAA of the Corporations Act protect eligible whistleblowers who disclose misconduct or an improper state of affairs concerning a regulated entity. Disclosures can be made to ASIC, APRA, an eligible recipient within the entity, or a legal practitioner. Public companies, large proprietary companies and trustees of registrable superannuation entities must have a whistleblower policy. Victimising a whistleblower is a civil penalty provision and a criminal offence.
Goods and services tax is a broad-based tax of 10 percent on most goods, services and other items sold or consumed in Australia. A business must register for GST when its GST turnover is $75,000 or more, while non-profit organisations have a higher threshold of $150,000. Taxi and ride-sourcing drivers must register regardless of turnover. Registered businesses report GST on their business activity statement and can generally claim credits for the GST included in the price of business purchases.
Section 588G of the Corporations Act imposes a duty on directors to prevent a company from incurring a debt when there are reasonable grounds to suspect the company is insolvent. A director who breaches the duty may face civil penalties and be ordered to compensate the company for creditor losses. The safe harbour provisions in section 588GA can protect a director who develops a course of action reasonably likely to lead to a better outcome for the company than immediate administration or liquidation.
The superannuation guarantee requires employers to contribute a minimum percentage of an eligible employee's ordinary time earnings to a complying superannuation fund. The rate rose to 11.5 percent on 1 July 2024 and to 12 percent on 1 July 2025. Contributions must be paid at least four times a year, by the 28th day after the end of each quarter. Employers who miss a quarterly deadline must lodge a superannuation guarantee charge statement and pay the charge, which includes the shortfall, nominal interest and an administration fee.
An employee who believes they were unfairly dismissed can apply to the Fair Work Commission. The application must be made within 21 days after the dismissal took effect. To be protected, an employee generally needs to have completed a minimum employment period of six months, or twelve months if the employer is a small business with fewer than fifteen employees. The Commission may order reinstatement or, where reinstatement is inappropriate, compensation capped at six months of pay.
Employees with at least twelve months of continuous service can request flexible working arrangements if they are a parent of a school-age child, a carer, have a disability, are 55 or older, are pregnant, or are experiencing family and domestic violence. The request must be in writing, and the employer must respond in writing within 21 days. An employer can only refuse on reasonable business grounds after genuinely trying to reach agreement. Disputes about refusals can be dealt with by the Fair Work Commission.
